{\LARGE\bfseries SINDYc world model: where it stands}

A small model of marine battles in SMAClite, found with SINDy, with Blue's chosen intent (advance,
hold the line, withdraw, focus fire) as input. It predicts the next few seconds well and every term has
a physical meaning. It cannot see how the units are arranged.

\section*{The model}

\textbf{State:} each team's health $H_B, H_R$ and the distance $d$ between the team centres.
Units alive $A$ and units in reach $R$ are worked out from them.
\textbf{Control input:} what Blue is doing right now, as on/off switches
$u = (u_{\text{adv}}, u_{\text{hold}}, u_{\text{wd}}, u_{\text{focus}})$. Exactly one of advance, hold the
line and withdraw is 1, the others 0; focus fire is 1 or 0 on top. Example: Blue holds the line with
focus fire $\Rightarrow u = (0, 1, 0, 1)$. In the equations a control multiplies a term, so it switches
that term on or off: $u_{\text{wd}}$ switches on ``withdrawing stops Blue's fire'', $u_{\text{focus}}$
switches on ``Red units die sooner''.
Health is on a 0--1 scale, where 1 is a full team of ten full-health marines (a larger team starts
above 1, e.g.\ 1.4 for 14 marines); units are divided by 10, so $R = 0.1$ is one unit in reach.

\textbf{Units in reach} \hfill \gamerule\ $\rho = 5.75$ \quad \designed\ 3 factors
\begin{align*}
  R_B = A_B \cdot s_B, \qquad
  s_B = \Phi\!\left( \frac{\rho - \text{gap}}{\text{spread}_B} \right)
\end{align*}
Units in reach = units alive $\times$ the share of them that can shoot. That share is an S-curve:
0 when the teams are far apart, 1 when they are close, and in between while the front units are in
range but the rear ones are not.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho = 5.75$: the game's attack reach.
  \item gap: distance from Blue's centre to Red's front units (Red's front sticks out ahead of its centre).
  \item spread$_B$: how spread out Blue is towards the enemy; a more spread-out team gives a more
        gradual S-curve, because its units come into range one by one.
  \item $\Phi$: the S-curve itself (the standard normal CDF). Red's share is the same with the teams swapped.
\end{itemize}
{\footnotesize In full: gap $= \sqrt{d^2 + \lambda\,(\text{width}_B - \text{width}_R)^2} - \beta\,
\text{depth}_R\sqrt{2\ln n_R}$ and spread$_B = g\,\text{depth}_B$, with three fitted factors
$\lambda, \beta, g$.}

\textbf{Health} \hfill \sindy
\begin{align*}
  \dot H_B &= -0.177\, R_R \\
  \dot H_R &= -0.186\, R_B + 0.163\, R_B\, u_{\text{wd}}
\end{align*}
Each enemy unit in reach removes about 0.018 health per second, about 8\,hp (80\% of a marine's full
damage).
Withdrawing removes 88\% of Blue's fire. SINDy kept 1--2 of 10 candidate terms per equation.

\textbf{Distance} \hfill \sindy\ coefficients \quad \designed\ switch shapes
\begin{align*}
  \dot d = {} & -6.01\, c_{\text{adv}}\, u_{\text{adv}}
             - 5.92\, c_{\text{adv}}\, u_{\text{hold}}\, \kappa
             - 3.18\, c_{\text{hold}}\, u_{\text{hold}}\, (1-\kappa) \\
           & - 3.16\, c_{\text{wd}}\, u_{\text{wd}}
             + 0.30\, f\, u_{\text{wd}}
\end{align*}
$\dot d$ is how fast the distance between the teams changes (negative = they get closer). Only one
intent is 1 at a time, so only its terms are active. Three helper switches, each between 0 and 1:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $c$ (\emph{closing}): about 1 while the teams are still apart and walking towards each other,
        falling to 0 once they are in contact and stop to shoot. One per intent, because each intent
        stops closing at a slightly different distance.
  \item $f$ (\emph{flee}): about 1 when a withdrawing Blue is close enough to the enemy to walk away.
  \item $\kappa$ (\emph{contact}): the chance that at least one Blue unit is in reach.
\end{itemize}
What the equation becomes for each intent:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Blue does} & \textbf{Equation} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
advance & $\dot d = -6.01\, c$ & both teams walk: they close at about $2 \times 3.15$ per second \\
hold, before contact & $\dot d = -3.18\, c$ & only Red walks: about 3 per second \\
hold, in contact & $\dot d = -5.92\, c$ & Blue's units move in to fight, as under advance \\
withdraw & $\dot d = -3.16\, c + 0.30\, f$ & Red walks in; Blue walks away when Red gets close \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The numbers (6.01, 3.18, \dots) are found by SINDy; the shapes of $c$ and $f$ were fitted beforehand.

\textbf{Units alive} \hfill form \designed \quad numbers \sindy
\begin{align*}
  \frac{d}{dt} \ln A_B &= 0.517\, \frac{d}{dt} \ln H_B \\
  \frac{d}{dt} \ln A_R &= (0.509 + 0.136\, u_{\text{focus}})\, \frac{d}{dt} \ln H_R
\end{align*}
Units die in proportion to health lost: losing 10\% of health loses about 5\% of units. Focus fire
makes Red lose units sooner.

\section*{How far ahead can it predict?}

Each curve is an average, not a single example: the model starts from every second of 1{,}000
validation battles it never saw (8{,}946 start points), is given Blue's real intents, and its
prediction is compared with what really happened. The real battle is the zero line: zero error.
For scale: a battle lasts about 10\,s on average (median 9\,s; 80\% of battles last 6--16\,s), so a
reliable horizon of about 3\,s is roughly a third of a battle.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{horizon_errors.pdf}
  \caption{Average error against the real battle, 0.5 to 8 seconds ahead. Health on a 0--1 scale
    (1 = a full team), units counted. Generic SINDy has no units-in-reach quantity.}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Health: about 0.055 off after 4\,s (5.5\% of a full team), against 0.23 when assuming nothing
        changes.
  \item Generic SINDy is just as accurate, but with 138 terms nobody can read.
  \item Units in reach is the weakest part: about 1--1.4 units off. Most of that comes from getting the
        distance slightly wrong.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Three example predictions}

Each column is one moment from a validation battle: the model starts from the real state, is given
Blue's real intents, and predicts 8\,s ahead. The three moments are not random. All 2{,}557 start
points with at least 8\,s of battle left were ranked by the model's health error after 4\,s (averaged
over both teams), and the moments at the best 25\%, the median and the worst 10\% were taken.
Each panel shows exactly 8\,s after the start, so no team is wiped out within a panel. Because
battles last about 10\,s, start points with 8\,s left come mostly from the longer battles.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{example_predictions.pdf}
  \caption{Real battle (solid) and model prediction (dashed), 8\,s ahead from time 0. Blue: Blue team;
    red: Red team; black: distance between the teams.}
\end{figure}

In the worst example neither team really loses health for 8\,s: the teams stay apart. The model
predicts that they close in and fight, so both health lines fall.

\section*{Rolling 4-second forecasts}

This is what a planner sees. Every second, the model starts again from the real state and predicts each
team's health 4\,s ahead. The three battles are not random either: all 807 validation battles with at
least three full forecasts were ranked by their average health error after 4\,s, and the battles at the
best 25\%, the median and the worst 10\% were taken.

\section*{Research questions}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.3cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Question} & \textbf{What works} & \textbf{Main limit} \\
\midrule
\textbf{RQ1} Can SINDy find a compact, interpretable model?
  & Yes: 1--2 terms per health equation, each with a physical meaning. Offered health as an
    alternative driver, SINDy drops it.
  & The distance law's shape is hand-fitted; a version derived from the game's walking rule did not
    work, because units behind their own front line are blocked. \\
\addlinespace
\textbf{RQ2} How does the state affect the model?
  & Team totals (health, units, distance, spread) are enough for the physics.
  & Team totals cannot tell a line from a column. That is why the model misses that holding the line
    beats advancing. \\
\addlinespace
\textbf{RQ3} How accurate is it over horizons?
  & Much better than ``nothing changes'' and than Lanchester (0.10 off at 4\,s).
  & Reliable to about 3 seconds. Over a whole close battle, small errors can flip the winner. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
